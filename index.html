Overview

The measurement gap

Modern agent deployments rarely consist of a single model. A coding assistant delegates to sub-agents for retrieval, editing, and verification; research prototypes organize dozens of agents into societies that negotiate and trade; and most real systems keep humans in the loop. How well these pieces work together decides how well the system works.

Our evaluation tools have not kept up. Standard benchmarks score one model on one task and say little about coordination overhead, specialization, communication efficiency, or whether adding another agent helps at all. Failure analyses of multi-agent systems point the same way: recurring problems in specification, coordination, and verification are not surfaced by task-level scores.

The first two WMAC editions asked how to make LLM-based agents collaborate. In their discussion sessions, one question kept coming back and stayed open: how do we know a group of agents is actually working well together? This edition takes that question as its topic.

Three questions we want to make progress on

  1. 1

    What is worth measuring in a multi-agent or sub-agent system, beyond end-task success?

  2. 2

    How can it be measured reproducibly and comparably across systems?

  3. 3

    When do collectives outperform individuals, and when do they merely add cost?

Call for papers

Topics of interest

Submissions on the following topics are welcome, but not limited to them.

📏

Metrics & evaluation methodologies

New metrics for coordination quality, communication efficiency, specialization, and robustness in agent teams. Extending single-agent frameworks such as AgentBoard, and multi-agent arenas such as LLMArena, to measure collective behavior.

🧪

Benchmarks, environments & simulations

Controlled environments where collective behavior can be studied reproducibly, including negotiation and social settings, and benchmarks that score collaboration and competition directly.

∑

Foundations & theories

Formal definitions and scaling laws for collective intelligence, with connections to how it has been studied in biological and economic systems.

🌳

Hierarchical & sub-agent architectures

Measurement questions specific to planner–executor and manager–worker structures, where performance is attributable to no single component.

🤝

Human–agent collectives

Evaluation of mixed human–AI teams and collective decision making.

🚀

Applications

Case studies from software engineering, scientific discovery, robotics, healthcare, and economics where collective performance was measured, successfully or not.
